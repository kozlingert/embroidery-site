Embroidery uses a variety of stitches to create texture, detail and decorative patterns.
Below is an overview of some of the most common stitches to help you master this craft!

Running stitch

Closing seams

The most basic stitch in embroidery on which all other sewing stitches are based. They are most often not visible.

Backstitch

Outlines, fine details and lettering

The strongest among the basic stitches, individual stitches are made backward to the general direction of sewing.

Satin stitch

Filling in spaces

A series of smooth flat stitches that completely covers a section of the background fabric.

Chain stitch

Embellishment near seams

A series of looped stitches form a chain-like pattern. They can form straight or curved, flowing lines.

French knot

Embellishment, filling in spaces

A decorative raised knot that adds dimension and texture. The knot can become larger if the thread is looped multiple times.

Split stitch

Outlines, lettering

This stitch creates a very even line every time the needle goes through the fabric. It resembles a narrow and smooth chain stitch.

Blanket stitch

Decorative and secure borders

A decorative stitch that reinforces edges and prevents the fabric from fraying.

Herringbone stitch

Embellishment, secure borders

A very tight weave makes this stitch strong and sturdy. It creates a texture and secures edges.
